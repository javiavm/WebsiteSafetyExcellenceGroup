Section 1: Electrical Safety Program (NFPA 70E)
1
Arc Flash Risk Assessment Complete
NFPA 70E Article 130.5
  • Arc flash risk assessment by qualified engineer
  • Incident energy calculations for all equipment >240V
  • Labels installed on panels, switchgear, transformers
  • Assessment updated within 5 years or after mods
2
Energized Electrical Work Permit (EEWP) Process
NFPA 70E Article 130.2
  • EEWP required for energized work >50V
  • Justified business necessity documented
  • Permits specify PPE category, AFB, approach boundaries
  • No "blanket permits" - task-specific authorization
3
PPE Selection and Availability
NFPA 70E Article 130.5, Table 130.5(G)
  • Arc-rated PPE available (Categories 1-4)
  • Face shields + balaclava or arc hoods for Cat 2+
  • Voltage-rated gloves per Table 130.7(C)(15)(a)
  • Glove inspection logs current (6-month testing)
4
Qualified Person Verification
NFPA 70E Article 100
  • Training records for all electrical workers
  • Training specific to on-site equipment types
  • Demonstrated competency (not just course completion)
  • Annual refresher training documented
Section 2: Lockout/Tagout Program (OSHA 1910.147)
5
Equipment-Specific LOTO Procedures
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147(c)(4)
  • Written procedure for each equipment type
  • All energy sources covered (elec, pneumatic, hydraulic)
  • UPS bypass procedures documented
  • Generator and ATS isolation procedures documented
6
Group LOTO for Complex Servicing
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147(f)(3)
  • Group lockout procedure for multi-worker tasks
  • Primary authorized employee designated
  • Individual lockout devices for each worker
  • Shift change procedures documented
7
Periodic Inspection of LOTO Procedures
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147(c)(6)
  • Annual inspection by authorized employee
  • Inspector different from person evaluated
  • Certification documents current
  • Deficiencies documented and corrected
Section 3: Fall Protection (OSHA 1926 Subpart M)
8
Elevated Work Plans for Overhead Cable Tray
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.502
  • Fall protection required at 6 feet
  • Cable tray work has specific fall protection plan
  • PFAS installed and inspected
  • Rescue plan documented and equipment staged
9
Ladder Safety Program
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1053
  • Ladder inspection program in place
  • Fiberglass ladders near electrical equipment
  • A-frame on level surfaces only
  • No metal ladders on site
10
Raised Floor Opening Protection
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.502(i)
  • All floor openings covered or guarded
  • Covers secured and marked
  • Proper tile lifting procedures trained
  • Tile carts/racks provided
Section 4: Hot Work Controls
11
Hot Work Permit System
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.352, NFPA 51B
  • Permits required for welding, cutting, brazing
  • Fire watch for duration + 30 min after
  • Combustibles cleared within 35 feet
  • Fire extinguisher within 20 feet
12
Fire Detection During Construction
NFPA 75, NFPA 76
  • Fire detection maintained throughout construction
  • Impairment notification process for outages
  • Alternative fire watch when impaired
  • Suppression systems protected from accidental discharge
Section 5: Contractor Safety Management
13
Contractor Pre-Qualification
Industry Best Practice
  • EMR verification (<1.0 required, <0.8 preferred)
  • DART rate verification
  • OSHA 10/30 training verified for workers
  • NFPA 70E training for electrical workers
14
Daily Safety Coordination
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.16
  • Daily coordination meetings held
  • All contractors represented
  • Hot work, LOTO, confined space coordinated
  • Work area boundaries defined
15
Multi-Employer Responsibilities
OSHA Multi-Employer Citation Policy
  • Controlling employer duties documented
  • Creating employer responsibilities clear
  • Right-to-stop-work authority granted
  • Hazard correction process established
Section 6: Emergency Response
16
Emergency Action Plan
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.35
  • Written EAP for the site
  • Evacuation routes posted
  • Assembly points away from electrical equipment
  • Plan addresses electrical emergencies
17
Electrical Rescue Equipment
NFPA 70E Article 110.2
  • Rescue hooks/blankets near high-voltage areas
  • AED within 3-minute response time
  • Personnel trained in electrical rescue
  • Local EMS briefed on site hazards
18
Spill Response for Battery Systems
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.441
  • Battery room spill containment in place
  • Spill response kits accessible
  • Workers trained on battery acid hazards
  • Eye wash within 10 seconds of battery areas
Section 7: Documentation and Verification
19
Safety Documentation Currency
Multiple OSHA Standards
  • Training records current (OSHA, NFPA 70E, LOTO)
  • Equipment inspection records current
  • EEWP and hot work permits filed
  • Corrective actions tracked to completion
20
Pre-Energization Safety Verification
NFPA 70E, Industry Best Practice
  • Visual inspection of all electrical connections
  • Torque verification documented
  • IR scanning scheduled for first 30 days
  • All workers cleared from energization zone
